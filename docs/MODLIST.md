## About this handbook

This guide starts with the systems that most change a playthrough and ends with the complete controls reference. Featured Mechanics gives the full walkthrough for major mods; Parts III–VI provide a quicker reference to the rest of the pack, grouped by what players do with them. Each entry names its core mechanic, offers a practical cue, and links to the mod's project page when one is public.

The alphabetical index finds a mod by name, while the gameplay-tag index groups mods by shared activities. Use those indexes to jump into a category, then follow the official page link when you need the complete item or configuration reference. Libraries, APIs, and invisible server internals stay outside the gameplay catalogue.

## How entries are organized

Each part opens with the role its systems play in a new world. Distinct category bars and inset icon entries make the relationships visible without repeating a full walkthrough for every mod. A short category paragraph closes each group; featured mechanics remain fully explained in Part I.

## Quick Reference

Use this page to begin a new world. [Featured Mechanics](#part-i--featured-mechanics) explains the major systems first; the complete [keybind reference](#part-vi--keybinds-and-configuration) is at the end for setup or troubleshooting.

### Major systems that change how you play

- **Build an RPG character:** choose a lasting race, assemble a flexible weapon-and-book loadout, then grow it through skill trees, spells, runes, and gear. Start with [Choosing a class](#feature-rpg-series) and [Skill trees and character progression](#skill-trees-and-character-progression).
- **Connect gear and progression:** Curios holds pack-provided accessories and the cosmetic layer, while Penchant lets eligible enchantments grow as you use their gear. See [Curios slots and vanity cosmetics](#curios-slots-and-vanity-cosmetics) and [Penchant](#penchant).
- **Explore with intent:** use the explored-terrain map to plan routes, then follow the Cave Codex and Cave Map to find Alex's Caves. See [Xaero's World Map](#xaeros-world-map) and [Alex's Caves](#alexs-caves).
- **Prepare for longer dimensions:** Astral Dimension branches into the Setback after its Void bosses; Chrono Dawn begins with a one-way portal until you stabilize it. Read [Astral Dimension](#feature-astral-dimension) and [Chrono Dawn](#feature-chrono-dawn) before crossing either portal.
- **Build a collection loop:** Starcatcher makes fishing depend on location and fish conditions, with gear, a minigame, and a catalogue. See [Starcatcher](#starcatcher).
- **Bring companions safely:** Pet Vault stores and summons owned pets, while Pet Status helps check their health and last-known location. See [Pet Vault](#pet-vault).

### First ten minutes

1. Compare the seven Iourus racial traits and choose a lasting race. Its direct menu shortcut is unbound; assign Open Race Menu in Controls to reopen the selection screen and read each option.
2. Equip a class book with a matching weapon. Changing the loadout changes your role, so try a few combinations.
3. Open the skill tree with `T` and read node requirements before spending points. Test your equipped spell or attack against an easy target.
4. Open the world map with `M`, mark a route, and keep coordinates visible with `N`. Check the Cave Codex before hunting for Alex's Caves.
5. Pack food and storage for the trip. For multiplayer, open Better Party from its inventory button before entering a dangerous area.

### Controls when you need them

The copied profile uses `T` for skills, `M` for the world map, `N` for coordinates, `G` for the emote wheel, `V` for the equipped backpack, and `E` for a combat roll. Better Party opens from its inventory button. In JEI, left-click shows recipes and right-click shows uses. Curios opens from the inventory button. If a binding conflicts, use the complete [controls reference](#essential-keybinds-and-conflict-resolution) at the end; press **Esc** while editing a binding to leave it unbound.

### Screen layout

Place Paper Doll at the upper-left with Coordinates Display immediately below it, and keep Better Party as a vertical roster on the right. This keeps the center clear and prevents those three overlays from covering one another. See [HUD changes and customization](#hud-changes-and-customization) for the layout diagram and setup notes.

<h3 id="feature-iourus-races" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/iourus-races.png" alt="">Iourus Races</h3>

- **Identity:** The installed build offers Human, Elf, Dwarf, Orc, Goblin, Melfork, and Draconic races.
- **Mechanics:** Each race changes attributes, passive strengths, and drawbacks; the choice affects play beyond appearance.
- **Progression:** Race is saved with the character, while class remains a separate equipment choice.
- **Decision:** Read each race's in-game traits before confirming. An ordinary player cannot freely replace a selected race.



**Choosing a race**

1. At the selection screen, compare every race's listed strengths, weaknesses, attributes, and passive traits. The installed build offers seven options.
2. Choose for the playstyle you want to keep. The author describes elves as mobile, orcs as combat-focused, dwarves as resilient, and humans as versatile; inspect the game for the exact current traits.
3. After confirming, build an equipment-based class around that foundation. A class can change with gear, but an ordinary player cannot freely replace a confirmed race.

A race affects survival and combat beyond appearance. Resetting one requires an administrator, so read the full selection before committing.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> Pick for lasting traits; you can change class gear later.</p>

<section class="feature-detail">
<h3 id="feature-rpg-series" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/archers.png" alt="">RPG Series</h3>

- **Classes:** Archer, Paladin, Priest, Rogue, Warrior, and Wizard are equipment-based playstyles in this pack.
- **Loadout:** Weapons and class books determine the abilities you can use; swapping them changes your active role.
- **Spellcasting:** Spell Engine supplies the ability bar, cooldowns, and rune costs for applicable skills.
- **Growth:** Specialized weapons, armor, and spell books deepen each archetype rather than locking your character to one class.



**Building a class loadout**

1. Choose an archetype: Archer for ranged play, Paladin for protection, Priest for healing, Rogue for mobile melee, Warrior for heavy melee, or Wizard for magic.
2. Pair its weapon with a class book. Use a Spell Binding Table to make books, then equip one in the Curios spell-book slot.
3. Hold the compatible weapon to reveal Spell Engine abilities. Follow the keys shown on the ability bar and carry runes for spells that consume them. Bypass Spell Hotbar is unbound in this profile; assign it in Controls if you want a manual override for the ordinary hotbar.
4. Add class armor, skill nodes, and accessories that reinforce the same role.

There is no permanent class-selection screen. If an ability fails, check the weapon, book, runes, target, and cooldown before changing the build.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> If the ability bar is empty, check both the class book and held weapon.</p>

<p class="feature-source">Mechanics source: <a href="https://modrinth.com/mod/wizards">official mod page</a>.</p>
</section>

<section class="feature-detail">
<h3 id="feature-astral-dimension" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/astral-dimension.png" alt="">Astral Dimension</h3>

- **Entry:** Build a Nether-shaped frame with at least ten Glowing Obsidian blocks and ignite it with Glint and Steel.
- **Exploration:** The Astral realm has ten biomes, dangerous Void Liquid, unusual creatures, and scattered structures; bring an Elytra and a safe return route.
- **Progression:** Astral Eyes, the Amethyst Sanctuary, Void materials, and the Void Egg lead to the Void Titan.
- **Next realm:** The later Setback dimension opens with Gloomy Obsidian and a Nanta Star, continuing the boss and gear progression.

**From the portal to the Void Titan**

1. Make the Glowing Obsidian portal, activate it with Glint and Steel, and mark your arrival point on Xaero's map. Void Liquid and broken terrain make flight and spare supplies valuable.
2. Explore the Fiery Forest for Burning Orbs. Their shards, four Glowstone Dust, and an Ender Pearl make an Astral Eye. Prepare at least four Eyes for the Amethyst Sanctuary.
3. At the Sanctuary, use the Eyes on the Postrum Eyes, defeat the four Amethyst Knights, and use their keys to reach Broken Amethyst Shards.
4. Mine Void Ore between Y -40 and -12 for Void Gems, and fight Void Menaces for four Void Fangs. Use these materials for a Void Pillar and Astral Altar, then place them at a Void Egg's portal to summon the Titan.
5. After the Titan, search the Tormented Mansion and its Void Visage encounter. The later Setback portal requires ten Gloomy Obsidian and a Nanta Star; enter with upgraded gear.

This pack contains an Astral Dimension 3.0 build ported to Minecraft 26.2. The public project page currently lists other game versions, so use the installed recipes and JEI for any details that differ. The two realms form a long expedition: establish a safe foothold, collect boss-gating materials, then tackle each structure in order.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> Set a map waypoint at the Astral portal before crossing broken terrain.</p>

<p class="feature-source">Mechanics source: <a href="https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/astral-dimension">official mod page</a>.</p>

<section class="feature-detail">
<h3 id="feature-better-party" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/better-party.jpg" alt="">Better Party</h3>

- **Groups:** Create public, invite-only, or password-protected parties with leader, officer, and member roles.
- **Cooperation:** Party chat, a live roster, member locator, and optional friendly-fire protection help groups travel and fight together.
- **Shared XP:** Nearby experience can be divided among eligible members when the server permits it.
- **Control:** Leaders set party rules within server limits; each player can adjust their own HUD.



**Playing as a group**

1. Open the party menu from its inventory button. Create or join a public, invite-only, or password-protected group.
2. Assign leader, officer, and member roles. The leader can adjust access and, when the server permits, friendly fire, nearby XP sharing, and the member locator.
3. Use `/p <message>` for party chat. Keep the live roster visible, and move or scale it in personal HUD settings if it covers combat.
4. Check the sharing radius before splitting up; members outside it may not receive XP.

Party membership can persist across restarts. Server rules set the limits, while each player controls only their own HUD layout.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> Set the roster where it stays visible without covering combat.</p>

<p class="feature-source">Mechanics source: <a href="https://modrinth.com/mod/better-party">official mod page</a>.</p>
</section>

<section class="feature-detail">
<h3 id="feature-the-sift" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/mielons-the-sift.webp" alt="">Mielon's The Sift</h3>

- **Entry:** An Ancient City portal opens through a Singer's melody and eight Note Blocks on Sonorous Deepslate.
- **Exploration:** The Sift has ten biomes, living sculk, unfamiliar creatures, abandoned portals, and restorative Ichor.
- **Travel:** Temporary Rifts connect the realm and Overworld for five minutes; do not depend on one as a permanent return route.
- **Rewards:** Siftite upgrades Netherite gear, while Charoite changes enchanting choices.

**From Ancient City to first expedition**

1. Find an Ancient City with Sonorous Deepslate near its Reinforced Deepslate frame. Play a Goat Horn in the city center to summon a Singer and awaken the eight blocks.
2. Put a Note Block on each awakened block. Right-click to set notes 1 through 8, then left-click the blocks in this order: **1, 3, 7, 6, 5, 2, 4, 8**. The order played matters, not where the blocks stand.
3. Mark the portal and enter prepared for a long trip. Follow the mod's advancement tab for its creatures, materials, and Ichor. Ichor grants regeneration on contact but slows movement slightly.
4. Search abandoned portals for supplies and rare upgrade materials. Use Siftite with Netherite gear and a Siftite Upgrade Smithing Template; compare Charoite enchanting against ordinary lapis before spending levels.

Natural Rifts may appear after time in The Sift, but they close after five minutes. Keep a reliable route to the Ancient City portal and use a map marker before exploring farther. This pack uses a community NeoForge 26.2 port and requires NeoForge **26.2.0.90 or newer**; check JEI and in-game advancements if a port detail differs from the upstream guide.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> The portal melody is 1–3–7–6–5–2–4–8; mark your way home before chasing a Rift.</p>

<p class="feature-source">Mechanics source: <a href="https://modrinth.com/mod/mielons-the-sift">official mod page</a>.</p>
</section>

<section class="feature-detail">
<h3 id="feature-better-archeology" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/better-archeology.png" alt="">Better Archeology</h3>

- **Sites:** New structures and fossil sites turn ordinary exploration into archeological expeditions.
- **Brushing:** Suspicious blocks and fossiliferous dirt can reveal artifact shards and fossil pieces.
- **Artifacts:** Combine shards into an unidentified artifact, then analyze it at an Archeology Table with a brush.
- **Rewards:** Identified artifacts can provide special enchantments; completed fossils can also serve as displays.



**From a dig site to an artifact**

1. Carry a brush while exploring ruins, old graves, and fossil sites. Look for suspicious blocks, pots, and fossiliferous dirt before breaking the surrounding terrain.
2. Brush promising blocks to collect finds. Fossil pieces come from fossiliferous dirt at their respective sites; artifact shards can come from pots and suspicious blocks.
3. Gather enough shards for an unidentified artifact. Use a brush at an Archeology Table to reveal the artifact, then inspect its effect and equipment requirements.
4. Assemble fossil pieces for a collection or display. Check JEI for the installed recipes before spending rare finds.

Bring spare brushes and mark promising sites on the world map so you can return after clearing your inventory. The mod adds structures to world generation, so search unexplored chunks for new sites in an existing world.

<p class="feature-tip"><strong>Quick tip</strong> Brush the dig site before mining through it; fragile clues may be lost when blocks are broken.</p>

<p class="feature-source">Mechanics source: <a href="https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/better-archeology">official mod page</a> · <a href="https://github.com/Pandarix/BetterArcheology/wiki">author's guide</a>.</p>
</section>

<h3 id="feature-penchant" class="feature-heading"><img src="assets/mod-icons/penchant.png" alt="">Penchant</h3>

## Part II — Character and Core Systems

A character develops through several connected choices. Iourus Races supplies a lasting identity, while RPG Series class books and weapons define a role that can change with the loadout. Skill trees, spells, runes, and combat controls then refine how that role feels in play. Read equipment as a complete build: an armor set, a Curios accessory, or a weapon effect may matter more than one displayed armor or damage value.

Progress continues through enchantment use, exploration rewards, and specialized gear. Survival systems also shape the build: a party can rescue a downed player, graves protect recovery, and food or healing changes determine how long a fight remains sustainable. The entries below are quick references; Part I gives the full walkthrough for the featured systems.

## Part III — Adventure and Challenge

Exploration is a chain of decisions rather than a list of places to visit. A map shows terrain already explored, compasses seek chosen targets, and waypoints or activated travel points protect the return trip. Mark home, check the current dimension, and carry the food, runes, arrows, and recovery items that the chosen build uses. A discovered structure or rare biome can begin an encounter with its own rules, so scout before activating unfamiliar blocks or summoning items.

World generation, seasons, creatures, bosses, and multiplayer events can overlap in the same journey. Newly generated chunks reveal new terrain after a pack update, while familiar explored areas retain their older layout. Read the local cues, record a retreat route, and prepare for the destination rather than carrying every possible tool. The categories below show which mod supplies each kind of travel, threat, or reward.

## Part IV — Survival and Creation

Survival turns collected resources into repeatable systems. Gathering provides materials, farms and kitchens turn them into supplies, storage keeps the result accessible, and building or logistics makes the base easier to use. Start with a reliable food and storage loop, then add specialized workstations, transport, and faster construction when the need appears. The right tool depends on whether the job is portable, local to a base, or shared with other players.

Recipes and workstations matter more here than memorizing long item lists. Use JEI to inspect the desired output and its actual station, then test a small batch before building an entire production line. Controls that affect bulk actions are noted where they help: OneKeyMiner uses `C`, Effortless Building opens its radial menu with Alt, and an equipped backpack opens with V.

## Part V — Interface and Reference

The interface is most useful when each layer has a clear job. Jade identifies a block or creature in front of you; JEI answers what an item makes or where it is used; tooltips explain the gear and food already in hand. Keep the permanent HUD for information needed while moving, such as position, health, and the party roster. Turning on every panel at once can cover the view during combat or building.

This section lists the installed information, recipe, and display tools as a quick reference. Use the mouse actions visible in JEI for recipes and uses, and consult Part VI before adding a shortcut that may conflict with another mod. Arrange HUD elements for the current GUI scale and resolution, then revisit the layout when a server scoreboard or larger party changes the available screen space.
